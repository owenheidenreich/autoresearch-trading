## Question

Volume profile's POC/VAH/VAL is the 70%-value summary of the volume-by-price
histogram. HVN / LVN is the richer structural decomposition:

- **HVN (High Volume Node)** = a local peak in the histogram. Price
  historically stalls/rotates here because it's an acceptance zone.
- **LVN (Low Volume Node)** = a local trough between two HVNs. Price often
  accelerates through these "air pockets" because no prior acceptance exists.

Hypotheses:
1. `abstention` (late-session setup): price stalled at an HVN → breakout
   setup → "near HVN" rate higher than control.
2. `entered_right` (ORC continuation): price crossing an LVN → momentum
   continues → "inside LVN" rate higher than control.

If either hypothesis holds materially, HVN/LVN adds signal beyond POC/VAH/VAL.

## Method

SPY volume aggregated into price buckets at 30/60/90/120-min checkpoints.
Smoothed with a 3-bucket rolling average before peak detection.

- HVN = local max of smoothed histogram AND bucket volume ≥ 2% of
  session-to-date total volume (prominence filter).
- LVN = local min between two consecutive HVNs AND trough volume ≤ 0.5× the
  smaller of the two adjacent HVN volumes.

At each oracle bar, measure:
- Distance from SPX close to nearest HVN (OMAR-range units)
- Distance from SPX close to nearest LVN (OMAR-range units)
- Whether SPX is "inside an LVN" (bracketed by two HVNs with the inner LVN
  nearer than either HVN)

Script: `v3/analysis/hvn_lvn.py`.

Sanity check: median 4 HVNs and 1 LVN per checkpoint. Some sessions have
0 LVNs (single acceptance cluster).

## Findings

### Finding 1 — HVN proximity does not discriminate

Near-HVN enrichment on abstention is 1.08× — within noise range. The
hypothesis that oracle bars stall near volume peaks is not supported by the
data. HVNs don't identify oracle-worthy bars above the control rate.

### Finding 2 — LVN proximity correlates with volatility, not quality

Near-LVN has **1.24× enrichment on abstention** — but **1.22× on side_error
too**. Both good and bad oracle outcomes happen near LVNs at essentially
the same rate. LVN-proximity is a "something is happening" signal, not a
filter that distinguishes oracle winners from oracle losers.

The absolute effect size (1.24×) is also weaker than OMAR (1.66×) or the
existing B+C stack (1.92×). Cannot replace or strengthen them.

### Finding 3 — "Inside LVN" is weakly anti-correlated with ORC wins

`entered_right` shows 0.82× inside-LVN rate — slightly below control. Clean
ORC breakouts do NOT happen inside air pockets; they happen outside them,
likely because by the time ORC fires, price has already crossed the LVN
into new territory. The "momentum-through-air-pocket" hypothesis is
backwards for ORC's time window.

## Conclusion

**HVN / LVN does not add signal beyond POC/VAH/VAL.** Near-LVN has some
directional-agnostic enrichment but cannot separate oracle winners from
losers. Near-HVN and inside-LVN are both at or below control rates.

**Do NOT add HVN/LVN as a filter.** The final teacher spec (B+C: VWAP +
OMAR retest at 1.92× enrichment) remains the strongest stack.

## What this does NOT rule out

- **Per-bar developing histograms (vs checkpoints).** Using a continuously
  updated histogram instead of sparse 30-min checkpoints might sharpen the
  node positions, though the signal being absent at checkpoint granularity
  suggests finer resolution is unlikely to help.
- **Multi-session composite histograms.** A rolling 5- or 20-day histogram
  might produce more stable HVN/LVN locations than a single session.
  Untested; low priority given the checkpoint result.
- **HVN as a TIME filter (not a price filter).** Measuring how long price
  has been near a specific HVN before a breakout might differ from simple
  proximity. Not the same question as tested here.

## Reproducibility

- Script: `v3/analysis/hvn_lvn.py`
- Uses SPY volume from the 1-min cache, SPX for oracle bar pricing
- HVN prominence threshold: 2% of session-to-date total volume
- LVN ratio threshold: ≤ 0.5× of the smaller adjacent HVN volume
- Smoothing: 3-bucket rolling average
