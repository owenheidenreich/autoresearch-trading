## 2. What counts as a significant breakthrough

### A. Trading-policy breakthrough — the primary finish

A complete policy, evaluated only out of sample, must simultaneously:

- choose an exact contract causally and be allowed to abstain;
- enter at the ask, exit at the bid and include measured fees;
- have positive executable net economics with a multiplicity-corrected one-sided lower bound above zero;
- beat an outcome-blind control matched on session regime, time opportunity, side, delta, premium and
  trade count;
- beat a shuffled-label null fitted and calibrated under the identical law;
- be positive absolutely and versus both controls in at least four of five chronological folds;
- survive feature-timestamp, mutation, survivorship, account and fill audits;
- produce a risk profile compatible with the declared $10,000 account; and
- remain positive under the complete frozen family, not a selected cell.

Only this bar supports a claim of a tradable candidate. It still does not authorize promotion, paper
orders or real money.

### B. No alternate trading game

The owner has fixed the product and position law. A spread, short-premium position, longer expiration,
futures trade, ETF trade or other strategic pivot cannot finish this goal. If the present evidence is
insufficient, the only valid data request is more causal SPXW 0DTE full-ladder history for the same
single-leg long-call/long-put model.

### Not breakthroughs

Prediction correlation, monotone deciles, a profitable pooled mean, one positive fold, a simulator,
another model architecture, infrastructure completion, a paper plan, or a newly worded blocker do not
finish this goal.

### Step 2 — predeclare before outcomes

### Step 3 — execute and verify

Implement with tests, call the applicable gates, run the smallest decisive experiment, and verify

the owner never relays their messages.

### Step 4 — adversarial audit

Treat every surprising profit as a likely bug. Check clock leakage, still-forming candles, current-day
normalization, contract survivorship, target clipping, selector attainability, cheap-contract bias,
spread accounting, duplicated sessions, regime drift, fold concentration and oracle contamination.

- policy evidence;
- stable predictive evidence without trading value;
- negative economic evidence;
- underpowered;
- specification defect;
- implementation defect;
- nonstationary/regime-specific; or
