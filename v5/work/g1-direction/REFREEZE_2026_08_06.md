# G1 family re-freeze, 2026-08-06

**One sentence: the frozen declaration was reissued because the machine's home directory was renamed
underneath it, and nothing about the experiment changed.**

The G1 family was frozen earlier on 2026-08-06 (commit `d3965c8a`) with content hash
`5ec8b5a46cf5cbeb7a36be14613288a88a3eea34612ec5d13926c6095caab742`. Later the same day the macOS
account was renamed `gduby` → `och`. Every absolute path into the old home stopped resolving, including

Because `declaration()` hashes that root, the dead path could not be corrected without changing the
hash. It was corrected, and both hashes are recorded here.

The root is now home-relative rather than absolute, so neither a further account rename nor the pending
[repo migration](../../governance/REPO_MIGRATION_MANIFEST_2026_08_05.md) can break it again.

## Why this is not a post-hoc widening

The freeze exists to stop the search space widening after a result looks attractive — the lesson of
ledger row 183. That risk is absent here, and the absence is checkable rather than asserted:

1. **No economic outcome had been inspected.** The order of work in [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) puts the surrogate
   known-answer campaign (step 4) before any real economics, and steps 3–7 are still outstanding. No
   member's profit and loss has been computed at any point.
2. **The diff is exactly one string, and it is not a research quantity.** A machine path is not a
   mechanism, direction, horizon, occupancy rule, clock, comparator, fold rule, or null. All 18 members,
   all 6 pass criteria, and all surrogate gates are byte-identical.
3. **The old hash reproduces on demand.** Substituting the old root back into the current declaration
   returns `5ec8b5a4…` exactly, which proves no second change is hiding inside the reissue.
4. **The corpus is the same corpus.** 261 files, and the two integration tests reproduce the frozen
   eligible index from the bars themselves — 254 M1-eligible sessions, 249 gap-eligible, and the four
   contract rolls on exactly the declared dates.

## How the discrepancy surfaced

It surfaced as a false green. After the rename, the two tests that read the real corpus were skipping on
`Path(family.ES_BARS_ROOT).is_dir()` and the suite still reported success — 142 passed, 3 skipped, with
the skip reasons ("owned ES bars not present", "owned corpus not mounted") visible only under `-rs`.

That is the more general lesson worth keeping: **an absolute path inside a skip condition converts a
broken environment into a passing test run.** The corpus checks in
[`test_reservation.py`](../../tests/test_reservation.py) and the G1 loader tests are now home-relative,
and the suite reports **145 passed, 0 skipped**.

# Second re-freeze, 2026-08-09 — the seven sessions the product cannot trade

**One sentence: seven sessions were removed because SPXW does not trade on them, so an edge measured
there could never be taken in the bot this screen exists to justify.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Old hash | `f43b92c2ed33a84e53732983999fdf889d31453cfe8663e3e09af1f182d8db49` |
| New hash | `157fe437998d968d41e7312a914dd65fa110b82c25600df0523c62a01699985a` |
| M1 eligible | 254 → **247** |
| Gap eligible | 249 → **243** |
| Excluded | 2025-09-01, 2025-11-27, 2026-01-19, 2026-02-16, 2026-05-25, 2026-06-19, 2026-07-03 |

## Why

ES trades a shortened session on US equity-market holidays; SPXW does not trade at all. Verified against
the owned option corpus on 2026-08-09: each of those seven ES sessions has **no SPXW option session
whatsoever**, and they are exactly the seven 13:00-close ES days. The two 13:15 early closes
(2025-11-28, 2025-12-24) do have option sessions and are kept.

The owner was offered the alternative of keeping all 254 and binding the constraint at G2 instead — G1 is
judged against ES friction and ES does trade those days — and chose exclusion, so that G1's number means
"tradeable in the actual product" rather than "tradeable in ES."

## Why this is still not post-hoc

**No G1 economics had been computed when this was decided.** The surrogate known-answer campaign had not
run; no member's profit and loss existed at any point. The decision was made on a *tradability* fact
about the calendar, verified from the option corpus, with no outcome in view. Deciding it after the
replay would have been exactly the row-183 failure this screen is built to avoid, which is why it was
raised and settled first.

The previous hash reproduces exactly when the index fields are substituted back, proving the diff is
confined to the eligible index and the member counts derived from it.

## What it costs

About **1.4%** worse minimum detectable effect (`sqrt(254/247)`), which is negligible against a screen
that can only detect a large edge anyway.

## The overlap worth remembering

`2026-06-19` (Juneteenth) is **both** a contract roll and a no-option session. It must be subtracted
once, not twice: 247 − 1 first session − 3 remaining rolls = 243. A test pins that relationship rather
than the arithmetic being asserted in prose.

## Bookkeeping

The excluded sessions stay in the **price chain**. The next session's overnight gap is still measured
against the ES close of an excluded day, because that is the close the live system would actually have
seen. Exclusion happens at eligibility, not at load.
