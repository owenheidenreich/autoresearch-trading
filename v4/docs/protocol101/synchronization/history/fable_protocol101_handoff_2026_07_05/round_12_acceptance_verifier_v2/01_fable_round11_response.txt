But the registry's perfect scores sent me into `processed_quality`, and two of the headline checks are weaker than their names, one of them vacuous:

**`full_ladder_share = 1.000` is an array-shape check, not a quality check.** The code counts rows where `option_ladder.shape[:2] == (21, 2)` — which is true by construction for every row the builder emits, including a hypothetical session of pure NaN quotes. It will read 1.000 forever on any input. That's partly my fault — Round 10's "share of minutes with the full 21×2 ladder" was ambiguous wording — but the intended metric is candidate *population*: mean tradable candidates per minute, and near-ATM tradable share (|offset| ≤ 20, both rights), each compared against bands calibrated from the 189 already-processed sessions. Keep the shape check, renamed `ladder_shape_ok`, as a sanity assertion; it just can't headline as quality.

**`labels_present` (`label_finite_share > 0`) passes on all-zero labels.** Your own builder test proves the failure mode exists: `compute_policy_labels=False` emits label arrays of zeros — finite, present, meaningless. If a batch ever ran with that flag wrong, every session would sail through acceptance and the folds would replay $0 trades. There's an instructive inversion here: your actual registry shows `label_finite_share` of 0.47–0.67 — NaN where candidates are untradable, finite where real — which is exactly what *genuine* labels look like; the all-zeros bug would present as a suspiciously clean **1.0**. Fix: add `label_nonzero_share` with a floor, and require both positive and negative label values present per session (real ask-to-bid policy labels must contain losers). The spot-recompute is the ground truth on top.

The rest of the run is genuinely healthy: `tradable_minute_share` correctly implements the gate-agnostic ≥1-tradable floor, context causality at 1.0 is the real thing, and the Oct 14 dip in finite share (0.47 vs 0.59–0.67) is precisely the kind of point the calibrated bands should adjudicate instead of eyeballs.

**One new finding your calendar work half-surfaced: early-close sessions play a different game.** `expected_decision_minutes` correctly returns 209/224 for Nov 29 and Dec 24, but the *contract constants* don't bend — `no_new_entries_after = 15:30` and `forced_flat_before = 15:55` are anchored to a 16:00 close, so on a 13:00-close day the entry cutoff never binds, the forced-flat deadline never binds, and labels truncate wherever the data ends rather than at a governed exit. Rather than making the contract close-aware (a versioned semantics change for ~3 sessions a year), pre-register the cheap answer now: the verifier flags `early_close_session = true` and the placement predicate excludes them from fold windows (report_only). In the owned range that's roughly Nov 29 2024, Dec 24 2024, Jul 3 2025, Nov 28 2025, Dec 24 2025 — immaterial to fold counts, and it deletes an entire class of edge-case bugs from both backtest and the eventual live rule.

**On the Round-12 ordering: your Option 1, made concurrent.** The strict guarantee you're protecting — no session admitted to folds before the deep checks exist — lives at the *admission* join, not the *build* step. Building rows is verifier-independent: the deep checks re-read existing artifacts, and a failed check rejects the session regardless of when it was built. So: resume the October→June build now as a logged background batch (it's the wall-clock bottleneck — 10 sessions took long enough to stop manually; 191 remain), and implement verifier v2 in parallel. The mechanism that makes this safe is the one currently missing: **per-session records and the placement predicate don't carry a verifier version.** Add `verifier_version` to every acceptance record and require `verifier_version >= 2` in `fold_placement_predicate` — that structurally prevents the v1 partial-October passes from being grandfathered in, and reclassifies today's 10-session registry as what it is: a smoke run. Concretely for Round 12: (1) resume build, background, chronological, per-month logs; (2) implement v2 = label spot-recompute + fee_model pinning, sampled context reconstruction, ladder-population metrics with calibrated bands, label sign/nonzero checks, early-close flag, verifier_version stamping; (3) run full-October acceptance under v2; (4) if pass, continue batch with per-month v2 acceptance through June.

Two specs so v2 needs no further round trip. **Label spot-recompute:** ~5 tuples per session, but stratify across the month so the batch provably covers each cell of {policy 0/1/2} × {stop-hit, target-hit, time-exit, forced-flat-capped} × {near/far offset} at least once — a uniform sample can pass while never exercising the forced-flat branch. Recompute from the raw CBBO path (ask at entry, bid path forward, deadline = min(entry + hold, forced-flat)), assert exact equality, and pin `fee_model` from the observed identity — expect it to confirm labels are gross of fees, which closes audit 1E and means the $20 stress remains the entire cost model, a fact the eventual paper-ready hurdle must keep covering explicitly. **Context reconstruction:** ~10 sampled minutes per session rebuilt independently from the raw SPX/VIX parquet — including the *derived* columns (VWAP, momentum, OMAR, range), since those feed the gates — with full-coverage reconstruction reserved for October only.
